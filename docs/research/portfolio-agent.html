Summary

The Portfolio Agent should manage each Book the way you would: start from what your approved strategies say, then use judgement on market conditions and the current portfolio. It may only move inside limits you approved, and every decision comes with the data behind it. Before building it, ash_backend and ash_new become one data and portfolio platform, so the agent sees the same data you see.

The design rests on four ideas:

  1. One platform, one data truth. ash_new is the platform: the Lake, strategies, Books, backtests and the ledger. ash_backend's unique part, the MarketSmith data, moves in as one more source of the Lake. Its old Portfolio Manager and alerts are retired.
  2. Strategies propose, the agent composes. A strategy's output is a list of candidates and exits, not the portfolio. The agent turns it into the portfolio: which candidates to take when there are more than the slots, how much cash to hold in this market, how big each position is, and when to trim.
  3. Judgement inside an approved envelope. You approve the Mandate (hard limits) and a Playbook (the judgements the agent may make, each with a range). Code checks every decision against both before it reaches you. A decision outside them never reaches the Order list.
  4. Evidence for every decision. Each change carries a Decision record: what the strategy said, what the agent did instead, which rule in your Playbook allowed it, the numbers it read, and what it expects to happen. A scorecard later compares the agent's portfolio with the strategies' plain output, so the agent earns or loses latitude on evidence.

This widens one decision in the current stocks PRD, where the agents may only subtract from the Rule plan. Your ask needs bounded additions too: choosing among candidates, and holding more or less cash by market condition. That is the first item under Decisions for you.

From strategy output to portfolio

The agent's job is the step between what the strategies say and what you hold. That step is written down as a Playbook: a short list of the judgements the agent may make, each with the data it must read and the range you approve. The Mandate's hard limits sit above it and never move.

Each Run works in five steps:

  1. Strategy plan (code). Each approved strategy runs as it was backtested and gives its buys, sells and sizes. This is today's Rule plan, kept unchanged as the baseline.
  2. Market read (code). Code classifies each market's condition from fixed measures, so the label is reproducible: index against its 50- and 200-day averages, breadth (share of the index above its 200-day average), distribution days, and MarketSmith's market outlook. The result is one of a few named Market conditions, such as Uptrend, Under pressure or Correction.
  3. Portfolio read (code). Code lays out the current Book: exposure, cash, each position's weight and profit, sector and theme concentration, the drawdown against the breakers, upcoming results dates, and correlation between holdings.
  4. Judgement (the agent). The agent reads all three, plus each name's dossier, and composes the Order list using only the Playbook's levers below.
  5. Check (code). Code verifies each decision against the Mandate and the Playbook ranges. Anything outside them is dropped, and that part falls back to the Strategy plan. The Brief says what was dropped and why.

How an exposure cut is carried out (decided 25 Oct): it first stops new buys. If exposure is still above the agent's level, it trims the weakest holdings first, by lowest RS Rating, with ties going to the one closest to its stop. The Book's own drawdown doesn't move the level for now; the breakers still apply at −10% and −18%.

Two rules keep the judgement honest. The agent never picks a stock no approved strategy surfaced, so every position traces back to a strategy you approved. And the agent's portfolio is always measured against the Strategy plan run alone, so its added value is a number, not a claim.

Evidence: the Decision record

Every place where the Order list differs from the Strategy plan gets one Decision record, and so does every Run's exposure level. A decision without a complete record fails the check and falls back to the Strategy plan. So no evidence, no change is enforced by code, not by asking the agent nicely.

Each record holds, in words you already use:

FieldWhat it saysExample
Strategy saidThe Strategy plan's orderCW2 India: buy 120 ABC at the open, 6% of the Book

Evidence must come from the Run's own inputs: the Lake, MarketSmith's snapshot, the Feeds and the dossiers. A figure that isn't in the inputs can't be cited, so the agent can't invent a number. Code checks each cited value against the inputs.

The Brief shows the records as short sentences under each Order list, with the numbers beside them. The Journal keeps them, and the weekly review scores each one against its Strategy plan order: money made or saved, and whether the expectation came true. The scorecard groups those scores by lever. A lever that keeps losing against the plain strategies has its range narrowed, as a proposal for you. A lever that keeps adding can earn a wider range, also only with your approval.

Guardrails and approval

The agent proposes, code checks, and you place the orders. That split stays even as the agent gets more latitude: the agent's latitude grows inside the Playbook, never around the check.

What code enforces, whatever the agent writes:

  • the Data gate and Hold mode: on bad data, nothing new opens;
  • the Mandate's caps and breakers, on the Order list as finally sent;
  • every decision inside its lever's range, with a complete Decision record;
  • every cited figure matching the Run's inputs;
  • on any failure, the Strategy plan goes out unchanged, with a note.

A switch per Book sets how much judgement is used: plan only (the Strategy plan goes out as is), subtract only (today's PRD), or Playbook (this design). A new Book, or a Book after a losing quarter for the agent, starts lower. The paper Book always runs on Playbook, so the agent's judgement builds a track record before it touches real money.

The agents stay read-only, with no broker credentials. Web searches name securities and events only, never your holdings or amounts, as the PRD already requires.

Research

The research backs a manager who works inside written limits, trades less than the signals ask, and logs and scores every judgement separately from the model. It is also blunt that a language model's trading record can't be trusted in a backtest. The full notes, with 30 sources, are in the agents repo at docs/research/portfolio-agent-research.md.

Roadmap

The stocks PRD's build order mostly stands. Consolidation folds into its data phase, and the Playbook replaces the subtract-only Portfolio Manager in its paper phase.

The agent's judgement can't be backtested honestly, because a language model already knows how most past market moves turned out. So the rule-shaped levers are backtested in Phase 2, and the judgement itself earns its record forward, on paper, in Phase 3.

Changes to the existing issues if you take this design:

  • 22 (MarketSmith): the fetch moves into ash_new and keeps dated snapshots, instead of calling ash_backend.
  • 32 (Rule plan): renamed the Strategy plan; it adds the Market read and the Book read as outputs.
  • 34 (Portfolio Manager): the Check accepts Playbook decisions within their ranges, not only subtractions, and requires a Decision record for each.
  • 39 (Desk): the analysts feed evidence into Decision records; they still can't add positions on their own.
  • 40 (Override scorecard): scores every Decision record by lever, not only subtractions.
  • 41 (Lessons): a Lesson may now adjust a Playbook range, still only with your approval.
  • New issue: the Playbook file, its schema, and its ranges per Book.
