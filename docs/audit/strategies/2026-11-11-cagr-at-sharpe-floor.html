The most CAGR with a Sharpe ratio of 0.8 or more

11 November 2026 · a study on ash_new (backend/portfolio-dashboard) · follows raising the Sharpe ratio across markets and the Pipeline audit

Finding. A Book loses its CAGR in its own rules, not in its strategies, and one rule is the trap: the first breaker, which stops all buying from a 10% drawdown. Without it and without the exposure level, the US Book goes from 8.7% to 15.3% a year with a Sharpe of 1.00, and the gain holds in three windows, 2008 among them. India gains less and less surely (22.4% to 25.0%). Hong Kong gains nothing. The three Books together go from 15.4% to 19.5% a year at a weekly Sharpe of 1.47. Nothing is changed: the exposure levels and the Mandate are yours to set.

  • No rule alone matters; two together double the CAGR. A buy a rule cuts is never tried again. The money waits in the cash fund until the strategy next trades that stock, which for CW2 is its sale months later. So taking one rule away leaves the next to cut the same buy.
  • The first breaker feeds on itself. It trips at a 10% drawdown and lets go above 5%. In between the Book buys nothing, so what its strategies sell is not replaced, it drifts to cash at 3% and has less to climb back with. It kept the US Book from buying on 30% of days.
  • The exposure level hides it. A Book held to 80% or 40% in stocks falls less, so it reaches −10% less often. With the level off and the breakers on, the US Books make less than as they are (9.1% against 12.7%).
  • The strategies have their own market gates. CW2 sells everything when its index closes 3% to 5% under its 200-day average. The Book's level and breakers stand on top of that.

9. Limits

The floor is met over the ten years, not everywhere. Every proposed Book is over 0.8 on the ten years. The US Book is at 0.74 in the holdout and 0.72 from 2007 to 2016, where the S&P 500 made 0.85 and 0.15.

  1. The settings were chosen with every window in view. The US result is the same sign and size in three windows and for five sets of Sleeves, which is the case for it. India's is not.
  2. The Mandate's drawdown limit is −25%. The proposed Books' deepest falls are −13.2% (US), −14.5% (India; −19.2% over 2009–2016) and −9.3% together. India without a level and without breakers reached −24.0% to −25.7%.
  3. No Book was run through 2008 in India or Hong Kong. Their gold ETF and cash fund have no prices then. The US's 2008 is one crash.
  4. Low Volatility 30 has no gate of its own. With no exposure level over it, a third of the US Book is always in stocks. Through 2008 the Book's deepest fall was −12.2%.
  5. The first months. A Book backtest starts from cash, so the short windows understate every Book and flatter whatever fills it faster.
  6. One path each. A Book is a few dozen positions at a time; two or three points either way is noise. The averages over sets are the firmer evidence.
  7. Survivorship, local currency, no taxes or brokerage, as in every backtest here.
