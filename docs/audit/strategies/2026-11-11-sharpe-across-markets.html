Raising the Sharpe ratio across India, the US and Hong Kong

11 November 2026 · a study on ash_new (backend/portfolio-dashboard) · follows the Pipeline audit · next: the most CAGR at a Sharpe floor

Finding. Tuning the strategies further does not raise Sharpe. What a Book holds together does. Changing the Sleeves takes the three Books together from a Sharpe of about 1.35 to about 1.6 over the ten years, and from 1.33 to 1.40 in the 2013–2016 holdout, almost all of it from the US Book. Nothing is approved, launched or changed in any Book: the Sleeve changes are proposals.

1. How it was measured

Cells are Sharpe / CAGR / max drawdown, in local currency, after costs.

  • A strategy's figures are from ash_new's backtest engine, with its idle cash at the market's risk-free rate (India 6%, the US and Hong Kong 3%), as a Book's cash fund pays it. Windows: 16 August 2016 to 14 August 2026, and the holdout, 1 January 2013 to 15 August 2016.
  • A Book's figures are from the Book engine, under the Mandate 7d9f896c659f and the Market condition as set. Windows: the ten years to 9 or 10 November 2026, and the same holdout.
  • The frozen window is 2006 to 2016 on the frozen panels, which includes 2008.

2. What was tested and rejected

Four general changes were tested on every market and both windows. None raises Sharpe across them.

ChangeWhereWhat it did to Sharpe
Volatility target (15% or 20%, over 20 or 60 sessions, no leverage)44 strategy versionsLower in India in both windows (−0.07 to −0.21 over 2016–2026, for every strategy); mixed in the US; higher only in Hong Kong's holdout

Risk-sized buys in CW2: a slot scaled down for a stock moving more than the median oneCW2 selected, on the engineIndia 2016–2026 +0.13; the other five cells −0.00 to −0.05
Buying each Sleeve back to its strategy's weights when the exposure level risesThe Book engine, five BooksIndia 1.28 → 1.31 with a deeper drawdown, holdout 1.68 → 1.54; the US and Hong Kong lower

The first two are worked out on stored NAVs, so they are estimates. The last two ran on the engines. With the earlier studies' 100-odd CW2 variants a market, the strategies look to be at their ceiling: about 1.1 in India, 0.9 in the US, 0.6 in Hong Kong.

The fourth is worth knowing whatever its result: the Book engine trims a Book when its Market condition worsens and never buys it back when the condition improves. A holding it trimmed returns only when its strategy next trades it.

The Books' weekly returns correlate 0.1 to 0.3 with each other, against 0.6 to 0.9 for two strategies in one market. That is why the whole is at 1.35 already with a US Book at 0.57, and why mending the US Book moves the whole more than anything done inside India. The best split found on the ten years is no better in the holdout, so the capital is left as set.

7. Limits

The Sleeve sets were chosen with both windows in view, from 17 sets run on the Book engine and 83 strategies screened. The holdout shows consistency here; it is not a test nobody looked at. The honest test is the paper Book.

  1. Steady Leaders was designed on all three windows, and its family has 196 trials. India's case for it rests on a smaller drawdown, not a higher Sharpe.
  2. The templates were not audited as the ash ports were. Low Volatility 30's market caps are approximate, and its sectors are today's.
  3. Survivorship: the Lake has what was delisted since it began, not before. Every figure is overstated.
  4. No Book was run through 2008. The frozen panels have each strategy's run, not a Book's. Low Volatility 30 alone fell 38% to 53% then; a Book's exposure level would have cut it to 40% in stocks, which is untested.
  5. A quarterly Sleeve under the exposure level: what the level trims of it stays in the cash fund until the next quarter.
  6. Local currency. A rupee investor also carries the dollar's and the Hong Kong dollar's moves.
  7. Taxes and brokerage are not in any figure, as in every backtest here.
